# Sub-4-bit quantization on Apple Silicon, and converting within ~20 GB of free disk

Ticket: [#3](https://github.com/ailabhq/model-fitter/issues/3). Researched 2026-11-23.

Code links are pinned to the commits read:
llama.cpp [`a0677c3`](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/tree/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830),
mlx-lm [`1bf8da0`](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/tree/1bf8da05a1ef30a3eb2e8a634180be6557fd3ca0),
mlx [`b835fb3`](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/tree/b835fb313e14d08de17d43cd59ce2dbfd3422569).
Anything marked **[INFERENCE]** is my reasoning from those sources. I did not run or measure it.

## Answer

1. **What runs at 2–3 bits on a Mac today:** GGUF k-quants and i-quants in llama.cpp's Metal backend, and MLX affine 2-bit and 3-bit, including mixed-bit and learned variants. NVFP4 is not a sub-4-bit format; it costs 4.5 bits per weight. MLX runs it on Metal. llama.cpp runs it only on the CPU on a Mac because its Metal backend has no NVFP4 kernels yet.
2. **Published Quality loss for ~27B models at 2–3 bits.** Numbers exist for both reference models, but only for GGUF. Qwen3.8-27B at 2.88 bpw (UD-Q2_K_XL, 9.8 GB): wikitext-2 perplexity 7.54 against 7.05 for BF16, and GPQA-Diamond 86.9 against 89.9. At about 3.5 bpw (11.8–12 GB) the results are close to lossless. Inkling 2 Mini at 9.1 GB (UD-Q2_K_XL): mean KLD 0.060 and 91.6 % same top token. At 13.6 GB (UD-Q3_K_XL): KLD 0.017 and 96.3 % same top token. I found **no published 2–3-bit quality numbers for an MLX quant of a ~27B model.**
3. **Converting shard by shard within ~20 GB: no stock tool does it.** llama.cpp has to write a whole intermediate GGUF first: at least 27.8 GB at Q8_0, or about 52–54 GB at BF16. `mlx_lm.convert` downloads every source safetensors file first (55.6 GB for Qwen3.8-27B). **A custom pipeline could do it.** Every method here quantizes one tensor at a time, and the source tensors can be read one shard at a time or through HTTP Range requests. Peak disk would then be about the size of the output plus one source shard. **[INFERENCE]**
4. **Calibration (imatrix) needs forward passes through the whole model.** But an imatrix is small (14–91 MB) and keyed by tensor name, and published imatrix files already exist for both reference models. DWQ needs the full teacher model, though its targets can be computed ahead of time and saved to disk.

Per `hf_quant_config.json`, the routed experts of 20 layers are NVFP4 (group 16, E4M3 scales) and those of 4 layers (20–23) are MXFP8; everything else is BF16 or F32 ([hf_quant_config.json](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini/blob/main/hf_quant_config.json)) |
| Shard layout | Mostly in layer order, but one layer can span two neighbouring shards. The embeddings are alone in shard 3, and `lm_head` plus MTP are in shard 18 ([index.json](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/blob/main/model.safetensors.index.json)) | One file, so "one shard at a time" means the whole 19.5 GB. Streaming has to work per tensor, using byte ranges |

So, for Inkling, every sub-4-bit Fit **re-quantizes weights that are already 4-bit (NVFP4)**. The llama.cpp converter first dequantizes the NVFP4 and MXFP8 experts to float ([`conversion/inkling2.py` L116–185](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/conversion/inkling2.py#L116-L185)). ggml-org describes its BF16 GGUF as the NVFP4 and MXFP8 experts "upcast to BF16" ([ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF)). Because that upcast is exact, Quality loss for Inkling can be measured against the BF16 upcast.

## Why no stock tool converts shard by shard, and what would

### llama.cpp

- Conversion happens in two phases: first "Convert the original model to GGUF format", then "Quantize the converted GGUF file" ([quantize README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/tools/quantize/README.md)). `convert_hf_to_gguf.py` writes only `f32, f16, bf16, q8_0, tq1_0, tq2_0` ([L60](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/convert_hf_to_gguf.py#L60)). The Python `gguf` package can only *de*quantize k-quants and i-quants; the `Q2_K` class has `dequantize_blocks` and no quantizer ([quants.py L406](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/gguf-py/gguf/quants.py#L406-L412)). So k-quants and i-quants need `llama-quantize`, and that needs the intermediate GGUF.
- `llama-quantize` opens every split of its input ("failed to load GGUF split", [llama-model-loader.cpp L606–682](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/src/llama-model-loader.cpp#L606-L682)), so the whole intermediate has to be on disk. For the reference models that means Q8_0 at 28.6 GB (Qwen) or 27.8 GB (Inkling), or BF16 at 53.8 or 52.2 GB ([ggml-org Qwen files](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/tree/main), [ggml-org Inkling files](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF/tree/main)). Every option is above 21 GB before the output is even counted. Re-quantizing from Q8_0 also needs `--allow-requantize`, which "can severely reduce quality compared to quantizing from 16bit or 32bit" ([quantize README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/tools/quantize/README.md)).
- What already streams:
  - `convert_hf_to_gguf.py --remote` reads safetensors "remotely without downloading to disk" ([L113–114](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/convert_hf_to_gguf.py#L113-L114)). It fetches each tensor with an HTTP `Range` request ([gguf-py utility.py L221–235](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/gguf-py/gguf/utility.py#L221-L235)) and always runs lazily ([conversion/base.py L158, L234–241](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/conversion/base.py#L158-L241)).
  - `llama-quantize` itself reads, quantizes and writes **one tensor at a time**. It writes a metadata placeholder, appends each tensor, then seeks back and writes the header ([llama-quant.cpp L1247–1308, L1276–1279](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/src/llama-quant.cpp#L1247-L1308)), using row slabs capped by `max_buf_size` ([llama.h L468](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/include/llama.h#L468)).
  - The kernel that does the work is the public `ggml_quantize_chunk(type, src, dst, start, nrows, n_per_row, imatrix)` ([ggml.h L2753](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/ggml/include/ggml.h#L2753-L2760)).
  - The Q*_K_M-style per-tensor type choice can be computed from tensor names and hparams alone with `llama_quant_model_from_metadata` and `llama_quant_compute_types`. This is internal API (`src/llama-ext.h`) used by tests ([llama-ext.h L48–85](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/src/llama-ext.h#L48-L85)).
- **[INFERENCE] A shard-by-shard GGUF path model-fitter could build:**
  1. Plan the output header (names, types, shapes) from `config.json` and `model.safetensors.index.json`.
  2. Stream each source tensor, one shard (≤ 4 GB) or one HTTP range at a time, through the converter's name and layout mapping.
  3. Quantize it with `ggml_quantize_chunk` and the matching imatrix rows.
  4. Append it to the output file, then patch the header at the end, the same way llama-quantize does.

  Peak disk would be about the output (8–13 GB at 2–3 bits) plus one shard (≤ 4 GB for Qwen; 0 for Inkling read by range). That fits ~21 GB. It is untested.

### MLX

- `mlx_lm.convert` resolves Hub IDs with `snapshot_download`, and its allow-patterns include `*.safetensors` ([utils.py L265–306](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/1bf8da05a1ef30a3eb2e8a634180be6557fd3ca0/mlx_lm/utils.py#L265-L306)). The full source therefore lands on disk (55.6 GB Qwen; 20.2 GB Inkling, single file plus MTP). It then `mx.load`s every `model*.safetensors` ([utils.py L462–469](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/1bf8da05a1ef30a3eb2e8a634180be6557fd3ca0/mlx_lm/utils.py#L462-L469)). Peak RAM stays low because loading is lazy and saving goes one shard at a time, but peak disk is the source plus the output.
- mlx-lm drops the vision tower and MTP for Qwen3.5-family models: "mlx-lm does not run the vision encoder or the multi-token prediction head yet" ([qwen3_5/checkpoint.py L56–59](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/1bf8da05a1ef30a3eb2e8a634180be6557fd3ca0/mlx_lm/models/qwen3_5/checkpoint.py#L53-L59)). The llama.cpp Inkling converter likewise converts "the text backbone only", with vision in a separate mmproj ([inkling2.py L103](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/conversion/inkling2.py#L103)).
- Inkling 2 Mini is **not in mlx-lm main yet**. The PR "Add Inkling 2 Mini model" ([#2086](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/pull/2086), stacked on [#2061](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/pull/2061)) is open. llama.cpp main has `inkling2-loop` ([src/models](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/tree/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/src/models)).
- **[INFERENCE]** RTN affine MLX quantization is per-array (`mx.quantize`), and Qwen3.5 `sanitize` is per-tensor. A custom loop of `mx.load(shard)` → sanitize → `mx.quantize` → `mx.save_safetensors` → delete shard therefore needs only one shard plus the output on disk. Learned methods (DWQ, AWQ, GPTQ, oQe) also need forward passes, which brings in the calibration question below.

## Does calibration need the full model?

- **llama.cpp imatrix: yes, the full model has to run.** `llama-imatrix -m model.gguf -f calibration.txt` evaluates the model over the text ([README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/a0677c341ba6bbfbbf74fa3c966f4dba74e1c830/tools/imatrix/README.md)). What it stores, however, is only the diagonal statistics of the activations entering each tensor, one value per input column. The original PR notes the result "is much simpler and smaller" than gradient-based importance and still gives "SOTA results for 2-5 bit quantized models" ([PR #4861](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/4861)). The file is small and keyed by tensor name. Published ones exist: `imatrix_unsloth.gguf` 13.6 MB for Qwen3.8-27B ([Unsloth](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/tree/main)), and `imatrix.gguf` 90.8 MB for Inkling, "computed from the BF16" ([ggml-org](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF)). A Fit can reuse one when its tensor names match the converter's. **[INFERENCE]** For a day-0 model with no published imatrix, the Reference device would have to run llama-imatrix on a GGUF it can hold. The full-precision intermediates do not fit in 21 GB, so it would mean calibrating on a lower-bit copy; how much quality that costs is not measured in the sources above.
- **MLX DWQ: the full teacher has to run,** but the targets can be computed ahead of time with `--targets-only` and stored, possibly on another machine. Only the quantized student is trained locally ([dwq.py](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/1bf8da05a1ef30a3eb2e8a634180be6557fd3ca0/mlx_lm/quant/dwq.py#L291-L376)). **AWQ and GPTQ** calibrate block by block but load the whole model lazily from local files ([awq.py L450, L560](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/1bf8da05a1ef30a3eb2e8a634180be6557fd3ca0/mlx_lm/quant/awq.py#L450)).
- **oQ: forward passes over the full model,** or over a temporary 4-bit copy when the model exceeds ~75 % of memory. It also has a "Sensitivity Model" option that "measures sensitivity on an already quantized copy of the model to use about 4x less memory" ([oQ doc](https://github.com/jundot/omlx/blob/main/docs/oQ_Quantization.md)).
- **RTN (MLX affine uniform and mixed, GGUF k-quants without imatrix, NVFP4): no calibration.**

## Gaps

- No primary source reports 2–3-bit **MLX** Quality loss for a ~27B dense or MoE model. The Quality-loss ticket should measure it.
- The Inkling KLD figures are measured against UD-Q8_K_XL or the BF16 upcast, not against the NVFP4 Source running natively. The two should be nearly the same because the upcast is exact, but no one has published a check.
- The shard-streaming GGUF and MLX writers above are a design inference from the code. Nobody has published or tested them.
